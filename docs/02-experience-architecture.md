# Experience architecture

## Purpose

This document defines the experience hierarchy and the routes from a known cultural interest to an unfamiliar Book or Film through human evidence.

## Experience hierarchy

1. Explore is the signed-in home and primary discovery surface.
2. Search begins from a Book, Film, idea, personal Theme, Thought, or person.
3. Media pages organize human routes around one shared work.
4. Thoughts and connections explain why a route matters.
5. Personal Theme regions provide conceptual routes through one person's public material.
6. Public profiles and Maps establish taste, authorship, and trust.
7. My Map lets the owner inspect, shape, and contribute to that public context.
8. Library keeps Saves, Bookmarks, and Drafts private.

The Map remains a distinctive core surface, but a new User does not need to build one before Contour proves discovery value.

## Primary navigation

The initial signed-in navigation remains small:

- **Explore**
- **My Map**
- **Library**
- **Profile**
- A persistent **New Thought** action

Search belongs prominently inside Explore and remains available across Media, Theme, Map, and profile surfaces.

It does not need its own permanent tab when that would duplicate the same entry point.

## Explore

Explore is an intentional discovery workspace, not an infinite activity feed.

It should let someone start from a recognizable Book, Film, phrase, idea, Theme, or person and receive bounded unfamiliar Book and Film candidates.

Each candidate should expose why it appeared and let the viewer inspect the Thought, connection, Personal Theme, or person supplying human evidence.

The viewer can Vote on route relevance, Bookmark the evidence, Save the work, follow a useful person, and continue into the surrounding Media page, Theme, or Map.

Recommendation reasons must use the strongest understandable evidence available, such as:

- `Because you Saved …`
- `Connected by someone you follow`
- `Found in Maps similar to yours`
- `Appears in personal Themes related to your search`
- `People whose Thoughts you upvoted also explored …`

The reason must match actual evidence, avoid another User's private behavior, and never imply a direct human connection where none exists.

Explore may contain a prominent known-work starting point, personalized Media with reasons, authored bridge Thoughts, relevant personal Themes from different people, people with distinctive Maps, varied interpretations, and a bounded editorial example for Users with little data.

Finite contextual routes and explicit continuation are preferable to endless scroll.

## Search

Search retrieves different object types without flattening them into one undifferentiated list.

For a query such as `grief and memory`, results may include Books and Films, Thoughts, explicit connections, visible personal Themes, and people whose public material engages the idea.

Each result should communicate what it is and why it is relevant.

A Personal Theme remains attributed to its owner rather than becoming a global tag page.

## Media page

The Media page asks: "What paths have people found through this work?"

It does not begin with an aggregate verdict.

The compact header includes cover or poster with a typographic fallback, title, author or director, year, Media type, public Like and private Save actions, and Add Thought.

Discovery content may include the viewer's own Likes and Thoughts, people they follow, Personal Themes containing the work, bridge Thoughts, different interpretations with clear authorship, recommended next works with understandable reasons, and people with substantial Map context around it.

Generic popularity is not the default ordering principle.

## Thought and connection detail

A Thought may open as a side panel from a Map or results surface on larger screens and as a full page on mobile or direct links.

The detail view shows the author and Map route, core statement and optional body, Media anchors, precise references when supplied, nearby Map or Theme context, correction history when relevant, later Thoughts that develop, revise, contradict, or clarify it, credited cross-person references, and applicable social actions.

Comments appear only if the selected shared phase includes them.

Connecting begins from an existing Thought and may attach another Book or Film, link another authored Thought, or create a new Thought referencing another person's Thought or Theme.

A public connection must contain authored meaning.

Spatial proximity alone never creates a semantic claim.

## Personal Theme region

A Theme first appears as a generated region in one person's Map after enough public works, Thoughts, and relationships support a coherent idea.

The system names it, and the owner can rename, hide, dismiss, or restore it.

A visible Theme on a public Map is searchable and directly linkable.

Opening a Theme shows its owner and Map region, supporting works and Thoughts, relationships, generated-name provenance when applicable, related or opposing personal Themes, and recommendations connected to the viewer's current interest.

Several personal Themes may be retrieved together without becoming a canonical global Theme or requiring manual filing.

## Public profile and Map

The profile establishes why a person's taste and interpretation may be worth following.

Recommended order:

1. Name, handle, image, and identity line.
2. Featured Books and Films from the public Map.
3. Visible Personal Themes when enough substance exists.
4. Interactive public Map with distinct taste, authored, generated, and external layers.
5. Featured Thoughts or connections.
6. Following relationships as discovery routes, without prominent audience counts.

Clicking featured Media opens its Map region or Media page.

The profile does not display consumption totals, streaks, completion goals, public vote scores, or audience-size status.

## My Map

My Map is the owner surface for inspecting and shaping public taste and authorship.

It should show deliberately Liked Media, distinguish Likes, Published Thoughts, private Drafts, and generated Themes, expose previous Thoughts, focus regions, surface recurring ideas, and preview the visitor composition.

The owner may connect a new Thought, rename or hide generated Themes, and move or explicitly pin appropriate nodes without creating authored meaning through movement.

Exploration progresses from Whole Map to a Theme region, Thought detail, and external context as needed.

External context reveals credited routes into another person's Map on demand, without importing that branch by default.

## Library

Library provides quiet private utility for Saved Books and Films, Bookmarked Thoughts, connections and Themes, Draft Thoughts, search, and basic organization.

Saving or Bookmarking does not publish, notify the source by default, or alter the public Map.

## First use

First use demonstrates discovery before asking for authored labor.

1. Show one strong seeded route from a recognizable work to an unfamiliar one through a real person's explanation.
2. Ask the User to select several familiar Books, Films, or existing personal Themes, without requiring writing.
3. Produce immediate bounded candidates with visible human evidence.
4. Let the User inspect a Thought, connection, Theme, and person's Map.
5. Let them Vote on the route, Bookmark evidence, Save an unfamiliar work, or Follow the person.
6. Let them Like familiar works to form a starter public Map.
7. Invite a free-form Thought or connection only after discovery value is clear.

The first discovery moment is: "I know this work; now I can see a human reason to try that one."

The first Map moment is: "The things I deliberately chose already form the beginning of something that looks like my taste."

## Return use

Contour supports natural cultural moments such as looking for the next work, finishing a work and wanting to respond, checking what others found in it, recognizing a connection, revisiting a trusted person or Theme, or refining the public Map.

Notifications and activity serve those intentions rather than manufacture urgency through streaks, autoplay loops, or artificial pressure.

## Responsive web behavior

- Desktop can place recommendation context beside Maps, Themes, and Thought detail.
- Mobile emphasizes search, candidate comparison, saving, voting, capture, and focused Map navigation.
- The complete graph need not be equally dense on a small screen; progressive focus is preferable to an unreadable miniature.
- Public URLs for profiles, Thoughts, Media, and visible Personal Themes are essential for discovery.
