# Experience architecture

## Purpose

This document defines how Contour moves someone from a known cultural interest
to an unfamiliar Book or Film through human evidence, then lets discovery and
contribution accumulate into a personal Map.

## Experience hierarchy

1. Explore is the signed-in home and primary discovery surface.
2. Search can begin from a Book, Film, idea, personal Theme, Thought, or person.
3. Media pages organize human routes around one shared work.
4. Thoughts and connections provide the explanation behind discovery.
5. Personal Theme regions offer coherent conceptual routes through one person's
   public material.
6. Public profiles and Maps establish taste, authorship, and trust.
7. My Map lets the owner inspect, shape, and contribute to that public context.
8. The Library keeps Saves, Bookmarks, and Drafts private.

The Map remains a distinctive core surface, but a new User does not need to
build one before Contour proves discovery value.

## Primary navigation

The initial signed-in navigation should remain small:

- **Explore**
- **My Map**
- **Library**
- **Profile**
- A persistent **New Thought** action

Search belongs prominently inside Explore and remains available across Media,
Theme, Map, and profile surfaces. It does not require its own permanent tab if
that would duplicate the same entry point.

## Explore

Explore is an intentional discovery workspace, not an infinite activity feed.

It should help someone:

- start from a Book, Film, phrase, idea, Theme, or person they recognize;
- receive unfamiliar Book and Film candidates;
- see why each candidate was selected;
- inspect the Thought, connection, personal Theme region, or person supplying
  human evidence;
- upvote or downvote the relevance of that route;
- Bookmark the evidence or Save the work;
- follow a person whose taste is useful; and
- continue into the surrounding Media page, Theme region, or Map.

### Recommendation presentation

A recommendation must show the strongest understandable reason available,
for example:

- `Because you Saved …`
- `Connected by someone you follow`
- `Found in Maps similar to yours`
- `Appears in personal Themes related to your search`
- `People whose Thoughts you upvoted also explored …`

The reason must not expose another User's private behavior or imply that a
behavioral candidate has a direct human connection when none exists.

### Composition

Explore may contain:

- a prominent search or known-work starting point;
- personalized Books and Films with reasons;
- authored bridge Thoughts;
- relevant personal Theme regions from different people;
- people with distinctive Maps around the current interest;
- varied or opposing interpretations; and
- a bounded editorial example for Users with little data.

It should not become an engagement-ranked chronological stream. Finite,
contextual routes and explicit continuation are preferable to endless scroll.

## Search

Search retrieves different object types without flattening them into one list.

For a query such as `grief and memory`, results may include:

- recommended Books and Films;
- Thoughts matching or complicating the language;
- explicit connections between works;
- visible Theme regions from individual public Maps; and
- people whose public material substantially engages the idea.

Each result should communicate both what it is and why it is relevant. A
personal Theme remains attributed to its owner rather than becoming one global
tag page.

## Media page

The Media page asks:

> What paths have people found through this work?

It does not begin with an aggregate verdict.

### Compact Media header

- Cover or poster, including a typographic fallback.
- Title.
- Author or director.
- Year.
- Media type.
- Public Like and private Save actions.
- Add Thought action.

### Discovery content

- The viewer's own Like and Thoughts about the work.
- Thoughts and connections from people they follow.
- Personal Theme regions containing the work.
- Bridge Thoughts connecting it to other Books or Films.
- Different interpretations with clear authorship.
- Recommended next works with an understandable reason.
- People with substantial public Map context around it.

Generic popularity is not the default ordering principle.

## Thought and connection detail

A Thought may open as a side panel from a Map or results surface on larger
screens and as a full page on mobile or direct links.

The detail view should show:

- Author and route to their Map.
- Core statement and optional body.
- Primary and additional Media anchors.
- Precise scene, chapter, passage description, or timestamp when supplied.
- Nearby Map or Theme context when relevant.
- Correction history when relevant.
- Newer Thoughts that develop, revise, contradict, or clarify it.
- Credited cross-person references.
- Upvote, downvote, Bookmark, and Follow actions as appropriate.
- Comments only if the selected shared phase includes them.

### Connect action

Connecting begins from an existing Thought and may:

- attach another Book or Film to create a bridge Thought;
- link another authored Thought; or
- create a new Thought referencing another person's Thought or Theme region.

A public connection must contain understandable authored meaning. Spatial
proximity alone never creates a semantic claim.

## Personal Theme regions

A Theme first appears as a generated region in one person's Map. It requires
enough public works, Thoughts, and relationships to support a coherent idea.

The system names the region. The owner can rename, hide, or dismiss it. A
visible Theme on a public Map is searchable and directly linkable.

Opening a personal Theme should show:

- the owner and relevant Map region;
- the works, Thoughts, and relationships supporting it;
- generated-name provenance when the owner has not renamed it;
- related or opposing personal Themes from other people; and
- recommendations connected to the viewer's current interest.

Theme retrieval may bring several personal regions together, but it does not
merge them into a canonical global Theme or require manual filing.

## Public profile and Map

The profile establishes why a person's taste and interpretation may be worth
following.

Recommended order:

1. Name, handle, image, and identity line.
2. Featured Books and Films from the public Map.
3. Visible personal Theme regions when enough substance exists.
4. Interactive public Map with clear taste, authored, generated, and external
   layers.
5. Featured Thoughts or connections.
6. Following relationships as routes to further discovery, without prominent
   audience counts.

Clicking featured Media should open the relevant Map region or Media page. The
profile should not display consumption totals, streaks, completion goals,
public vote scores, or audience-size status.

## My Map

My Map is the owner surface for inspecting and shaping public taste and
authorship.

It should help the owner:

- see deliberately Liked Books and Films;
- distinguish public Likes, Published Thoughts, private Drafts, and generated
  Themes;
- find previous Thoughts;
- focus a region;
- notice recurring ideas;
- connect a new Thought to an existing work or Thought;
- rename, hide, or dismiss generated Themes;
- move and explicitly pin appropriate nodes without authoring meaning through
  movement; and
- preview the same public composition visitors see.

### Exploration levels

#### Whole Map

Shows recognizable Books and Films, important Thoughts, explicit connections,
and visible personal Theme regions.

#### Theme region

Focuses one conceptual neighborhood and the evidence supporting it.

#### Thought

Opens the authored statement, Media anchors, nearby context, relationships, and
social actions.

#### External context

Reveals credited references and routes into other people's Maps on demand.
Another person's branch is not imported into the owner Map by default.

## Library

The Library provides quiet private utility:

- Saved Books and Films.
- Bookmarked Thoughts, connections, and personal Theme regions.
- Draft Thoughts.
- Search and basic organization.

Saving or Bookmarking does not publish, notify the source by default, or alter
the public Map.

## First use

First use must demonstrate the discovery promise before asking for authored
labor.

### Recommended flow

1. Show one excellent seeded route: a surprising connection between
   recognizable works, explained by a real person.
2. Ask the User to select several familiar Books, Films, or existing personal
   Theme regions they care about. No writing is required.
3. Produce immediate recommendation candidates with visible human evidence.
4. Let the User inspect a Thought, connection, Theme region, and person's Map.
5. Let them Vote on the route, Bookmark evidence, Save an unfamiliar work, or
   Follow the person.
6. Let them deliberately Like familiar works to form a starter public Map.
7. Only then invite a free-form Thought or connection.

### First discovery moment

> I know this work; now I can see a human reason to try that one.

### First Map moment

> The things I deliberately chose already form the beginning of something that
> looks like my taste.

## Return use

Contour should support natural cultural moments rather than daily compulsion:

- looking for the next Book or Film;
- finishing a work and wanting to say something;
- checking what other people found in it;
- recognizing a connection to another work;
- revisiting a person or Theme region worth trusting; or
- refining one's public Map.

Notifications and activity should serve those intentions, not manufacture
urgency.

## Responsive web behavior

- Desktop can show recommendation context beside Maps, Theme regions, and
  Thought detail.
- Mobile emphasizes search, candidate comparison, saving, voting, capture, and
  focused Map navigation.
- The complete graph need not be equally dense on a small screen; progressive
  focus is preferable to an unreadable miniature.
- Public URLs for profiles, Thoughts, Media, and visible personal Theme regions
  are essential for real discovery.
